The Discourse on the Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dhamma.

Thus
by me
was heard.
At one time
the Blessed One

in the city of Varanasi,
was dwelling
at Isipatana,
in the Deer Park.

There, indeed,
the Blessed One
the group of five monks
addressed:

Monks, there are these two extremes
by one who has gone forth
that should not be pursued.

Whatever is this
among sensual pleasures,
the pursuit of sensual happiness,
which is low,
vulgar,
practiced by ordinary worldlings,
ignoble,
and unbeneficial.

And whatever is this
pursuit of self-mortification,
which is painful,
ignoble,
and unbeneficial.

Monks, these two extremes
without approaching,
the middle way
by the Tathagata
has been fully awakened to.

Producing vision,
producing knowledge,
leading to peace,
to direct knowledge,
to full enlightenment,
to Nibbana,
it leads.

And what, monks, is that middle way
by the Tathagata
fully awakened to,

producing vision,
producing knowledge,
leading to peace,
to direct knowledge,
to full enlightenment,
to Nibbana
does it lead?

It is this Noble Eightfold Path,
namely:

Right View,
Right Intention,
Right Speech,
Right Action,

Right Livelihood,
Right Effort,
Right Mindfulness,
and Right Concentration.

This, monks, is that middle way
by the Tathagata
fully awakened to,

producing vision,
producing knowledge,
leading to peace,
to direct knowledge,
to full enlightenment,
to Nibbana,
it leads.

Now this, indeed,
monks,
is the Noble Truth of Suffering.

Birth is suffering,
aging is suffering,
illness is suffering,
death is suffering.

Association with the displeasing is suffering,
separation from the pleasing is suffering.

Not getting what one desires,
that too is suffering.
In brief,
the five aggregates of clinging
are suffering.

Now this, indeed,
monks,
is the Noble Truth of the Origin of Suffering.

It is this craving
which leads to renewed existence,
accompanied by delight and lust,
finding delight here and there,
namely:

Craving for sensual pleasures,
craving for existence,
and craving for non-existence.

Now this, indeed,
monks,
is the Noble Truth of the Cessation of Suffering.

It is the remainderless fading away
and cessation of that same craving,

the giving up,
relinquishment,
liberation,
and non-attachment to it.

Now this, indeed,
monks,
is the Noble Truth of the Path Leading to the Cessation of Suffering.

It is this Noble Eightfold Path,
namely:

Right View,
Right Intention,
Right Speech,
Right Action,

Right Livelihood,
Right Effort,
Right Mindfulness,
and Right Concentration.

"This is the Noble Truth of Suffering",
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of Suffering
should be fully understood,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of Suffering
has been fully understood,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

"This is the Noble Truth of the Origin of Suffering",
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of the Origin of Suffering
should be abandoned,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of the Origin of Suffering
has been abandoned,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

"This is the Noble Truth of the Cessation of Suffering",
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of the Cessation of Suffering
should be realized,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of the Cessation of Suffering
has been realized,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

"This is the Noble Truth of the Path Leading to the Cessation of Suffering",
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this Noble Truth of the Path Leading to the Cessation of Suffering
should be developed,
to me, monks,
formerly
regarding things unheard before,

Vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

Now, this noble truth of the cessation of suffering
has been developed
to me, O monks,
formerly
in regards to things unheard before.

Vision arose,
knowledge arose,
wisdom arose,
true knowledge arose,
light arose.

And as long as, O monks, to me
regarding these Four Noble Truths

thus,
with its three phases
and twelve aspects,
the knowledge and vision of things as they really are
was not thoroughly purified;

so long, O monks, I did not claim
with its devas,
in the world
with its Maras,
with its Brahmas,

with its ascetics and brahmins,
among the population
with its devas and humans,

to have awakened to the unsurpassed perfect enlightenment.

But when, O monks, to me
regarding these Four Noble Truths

thus,
with its three phases
and twelve aspects,
the knowledge and vision of things as they really are
was thoroughly purified;

then, O monks, I
with its devas,
in the world
with its Maras,
with its Brahmas,

with its ascetics and brahmins,
among the population
with its devas and humans,

claimed to have awakened to the unsurpassed perfect enlightenment.

And the knowledge and vision
arose in me:
'Unshakable is my liberation of mind,
this is the final birth,
now there is no more renewed existence.'

The Blessed One said this.
Elated in mind,
the monks of the group of five
the words of the Blessed One
delighted in.

And while this discourse
was being spoken,
to Venerable Kondañña

the dustless,
stainless
Eye of Dhamma
arose:

'Whatever
is of the nature to arise,
all that
is of the nature to cease.'

And when the Blessed One had set in motion
the Wheel of Dhamma,
the earth-bound devas
raised the cry:

'This
by the Blessed One
at Varanasi
in the Deer Park at Isipatana

unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma
has been set in motion,
which cannot be stopped

by any ascetic
or brahmin
or deva
or Mara
or Brahma
or by anyone
in the world.'

Hearing the cry of the earth-bound devas,
having heard the cry,
the devas of the Four Great Kings
raised the cry.

Of the devas of the Four Great Kings
having heard the cry,
the Tavatimsa devas
raised the cry.

Of the Tavatimsa devas
having heard the cry,
the Yama devas
raised the cry.

Of the Yama devas
having heard the cry,
the Tusita devas
raised the cry.

Of the Tusita devas
having heard the cry,
the Nimmanarati devas
raised the cry.

Of the Nimmanarati devas
having heard the cry,
the Paranimmitavasavatti devas
raised the cry.

Of the Paranimmitavasavatti devas
having heard the cry,
the Brahmaparisajja devas
raised the cry.

Of the Brahmaparisajja devas
having heard the cry,
the Brahmapurohita devas
raised the cry.

Of the Brahmapurohita devas
having heard the cry,
the Maha Brahma devas
raised the cry.

Of the Maha Brahma devas
having heard the cry,
the Parittabha devas
raised the cry.

Of the Parittabha devas
having heard the cry,
the Appamanabha devas
raised the cry.

Of the Appamanabha devas
having heard the cry,
the Abhassara devas
raised the cry.

Of the Abhassara devas
having heard the cry,
the Parittasubha devas
raised the cry.

Of the Parittasubha devas
having heard the cry,
the Appamanasubha devas
raised the cry.

Of the Appamanasubha devas
having heard the cry,
the Subhakinhaka devas
raised the cry.

Of the Subhakinhaka devas
having heard the cry,
the Vehapphala devas
raised the cry.

Of the Vehapphala devas
having heard the cry,
the Aviha devas
raised the cry.

Of the Aviha devas
having heard the cry,
the Atappa devas
raised the cry.

Of the Atappa devas
having heard the cry,
the Sudassa devas
raised the cry.

Of the Sudassa devas
having heard the cry,
the Sudassi devas
raised the cry.

Of the Sudassi devas
having heard the cry,
the Akanittha devas
raised the cry.

'This
by the Blessed One
at Varanasi
in the Deer Park at Isipatana
unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma
has been set in motion,
which cannot be stopped

by any ascetic
or brahmin
or deva
or Mara
or Brahma
or by anyone
in the world.'

Thus
at that moment,
at that instant,
as far as the Brahma world,
the sound
surged upwards.

And this ten-thousandfold world system
shook,
quivered,
and trembled.

And a boundless,
magnificent
light
in the world
appeared,
surpassing
the divine majesty of the devas.

Then
the Blessed One
an inspired utterance
exclaimed:

'Indeed, Kondañña has understood!
Indeed, Kondañña has understood!'

In this way,
to Venerable Kondañña,
'Anna Kondañña' (Kondañña who has understood)
the name
came to be.
