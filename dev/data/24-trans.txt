The Great Assembly Discourse

Thus have I heard:
On one occasion
the Blessed One

among the Sakyans
was dwelling
near Kapilavatthu
in the Great Wood
together with a great Sangha of monks

numbering about five hundred monks
all of whom were Arahants.

From ten thousand world systems
devas
for the most part
had assembled
the Blessed One
to see
and the Sangha of monks.

Then
to four devas of the Pure Abodes
this thought occurred:

This Blessed One
among the Sakyans
is dwelling
near Kapilavatthu
in the Great Wood
together with a great Sangha of monks
numbering about five hundred monks
all of whom are Arahants.

Devas from ten thousand world systems
for the most part
are assembled
the Blessed One
to see
and the Sangha of monks.

What if
we too
where
the Blessed One is,
were to approach him.

Having approached
in the presence of the Blessed One
a verse each
we were to speak.

Then
those devas
just as
a strong man

a bent arm
would stretch out,
or an outstretched arm
would bend,

in the exact same way
from among the devas of the Pure Abodes
they disappeared
and in front of the Blessed One
they appeared.

Then
those devas
to the Blessed One
having paid homage
stood to one side.

Standing to one side,
one deva
in the presence of the Blessed One
recited this verse:

A great assembly
in the forest,
the hosts of devas
have assembled.

We have come
to this Dhamma assembly
to see
the invincible Sangha.

Then
another deva
in the presence of the Blessed One
this verse
recited:

There
the monks
have concentrated
their own minds,
making them straight.

Like a charioteer
holding the reins,
their faculties
they guard,
the wise ones.

Then
another deva
in the presence of the Blessed One
this verse
recited:

Having cut
the stakes,
having cut the crossbars,
the boundary pillar (of lust, hate, and delusion)
having uprooted,
they are free from craving.

They
wander
pure
and stainless,
by the One with Vision
well-tamed,
the young Arahants.

Then
another deva
in the presence of the Blessed One
this verse
recited:

Whoever
to the Buddha for refuge
have gone,
will not
they
go
to the planes of misery.

Upon abandoning
the human body,
the hosts of devas
they will fulfill.

Then
the Blessed One
the monks
addressed:

For the most part,
monks,
from the ten thousand world systems
devas
have assembled
the Tathagata
to see,
and the Sangha of monks.

Those who
monks,
were
in past times
Arahants,
Fully Enlightened Buddhas,

for those Blessed Ones too
only to this extent
devas
had assembled,
just as they have for me now.

Those who
monks,
will be
in future times
Arahants,
Fully Enlightened Buddhas,

for those Blessed Ones too
only to this extent
devas
will assemble,
just as they have for me now.

I will declare,
monks,
the names of the deva hosts.

I will proclaim,
monks,
the names of the deva hosts.

I will teach,
monks,
the names of the deva hosts.

Listen to it,
pay close attention,
I will speak.

"Yes, venerable sir,"
those monks
to the Blessed One
replied.
The Blessed One
said this:

I will recite verses,
where
the earth-bound devas
abide.

Those (monks) who
dwell
in mountain caves,
resolute,
and concentrated,

like many hidden lions,
overcoming fear and horripilation,

with pure minds,
clean,
serene,
and unclouded,

numbering over five hundred,
having known,
in the forest
near Kapilavatthu.

Then
addressed
the Teacher
the disciples
who delight in the dispensation:

The hosts of devas
have arrived,
perceive them,
O monks.

And they
made an effort,
having heard
the Buddha's instruction.

In them
arose
the knowledge
of seeing non-humans.

Some
saw a hundred,
a thousand,
and then
seventy thousand.

Some saw a hundred thousand
non-humans.

Some saw countless numbers,
all directions
were filled with them.

And all of that
having clearly known
and investigated,
the One with Vision (the Buddha)

Then
addressed
the Teacher
the disciples
who delight in the dispensation:

The hosts of devas
have arrived,
perceive them,
O monks.

Those
I to you
will proclaim
in words
in due order.

Seven thousand
yakkhas,
earth-bound
inhabitants of Kapilavatthu,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

Six thousand
from the Himalayas,
yakkhas
of various complexions,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

From Mount Satagira,
three thousand
yakkhas
of various complexions,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

Thus these
sixteen thousand
yakkhas
of various complexions,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

The Vessamittas,
five hundred
yakkhas
of various complexions,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

Kumbhira,
of Rajagaha,
on Mount Vepulla
is his dwelling.

More than
him
a hundred thousand
yakkhas
attend upon.

Kumbhira,
of Rajagaha,
he too came
to the assembly
in the forest.

The Eastern
direction
the king
Dhatarattha
rules.

Of the gandhabbas
the lord,
a great king
full of glory
is he.

And his sons
his
are many,
named Inda,
of great power,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

The Southern
direction
the king
Virulha
that direction
rules.

Of the kumbhandas
the lord,
a great king
full of glory
is he.

And his sons
his
are many,
named Inda,
of great power,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

The Western
direction
the king
Virupakkha
rules.

Of the nagas
the lord,
a great king
full of glory
is he.

And his sons
his
are many,
named Inda,
of great power,

possessing psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in complexion,
and full of glory.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the forest.

And the Northern
direction,
the king,
Kuvera, also known as Vessavana,
that direction
he rules over.

of the Yakkhas,
the sovereign lord,
a Great King,
endowed with glory,
is he.

And his sons,
his
are many,
sharing the name Inda,
of great strength.

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

In the Eastern direction,
King Dhatarattha,
in the Southern direction,
King Virulhaka,

in the Western direction,
King Virupakkha,
and King Kuvera
in the Northern direction.

These Four Great Kings,
all around
the four directions,

shining brilliantly,
they stood
in the great forest
of Kapilavatthu.

Their
illusion-wielding
vassals,
came,
deceptive
and crafty.

Maya,
Kutendu,
Vetendu,
and Vitucca,
along with Vituda.

Candana,
and Kamasettha,
Kinnughandu,
and Nighandu also came.

Panada,
and Opamanna,
and the divine charioteer,
Matali.

And Cittasena,
the Gandhabba,
King Nala,
and Janesabha.

Came,
and Pancasikha,
Timbaru,
and the radiant Suriyavaccasa also came.

And these,
and other kings,
Gandhabbas,
together with their kings.

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

Then came
the Nabhasa Nagas,
the Vesali Nagas,
together with the Tacchakas.

The Kambala and Assatara Nagas
came,
the Payaga Nagas
along with their relatives.

The Yamuna Nagas
and the Dhatarattha Nagas,
came.
These Nagas
endowed with glory.

The Great Naga Eravana,
he too came
to the assembly
in the great forest.

Those who swoop down on Naga kings,
forcibly carrying them away,
the divine,
twice-born birds,
the winged creatures,
with pure vision.

Through the sky
they
arrived in the midst of the forest.
'Cittra' and 'Supanna'
are their names.

At that time, there was safety
for the Naga kings;
from the Garudas,
the Buddha granted them security.

With gentle words,
conversing with one another,
the Nagas
and Garudas
took refuge in the Buddha.

Defeated
by the thunderbolt-wielding Sakka,
the Asuras dwelling in the ocean,

these brothers of Vasava
endowed with psychic powers,
and possessed of glory.

The Kalakanjas,
of terrifying form,
the Asuras,
and the Danaveghasas;

Vepacitti,
and Sucitti,
Paharada,
along with Namuci also came.

And a hundred sons of Bali,
all
bearing the name Veroca,

having equipped themselves,
with their mighty army,
approached the auspicious Rahu,

saying, 'Now is the time,
blessings be upon you,
of the monks
to the assembly
in the great forest.'

The deities of Water
and Earth,
of Fire,
and of Air,
came there.

The Varuna deities,
the Varuna deities,
Soma,
and Yasasa.

The deities belonging to the groups of Loving-kindness and Compassion
came,
these deities
endowed with glory.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

The Venhu deities,
and the Sahali,
and the Asama,
and the two Yama deities;

The deities attending on the moon god
came, placing the moon god at the forefront.

The deities attending on the sun god
came, placing the sun god at the forefront.

Placing the constellations at the forefront,
came
the Mandavalahaka deities.

Of the Vasu deities,
the foremost is Vasava,
Sakka too came,
known as Purindada.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

Then came
the Sahabhu deities,
blazing like crests of fire.

The Aritthaka deities
and the Roja deities,
radiant like the umma flower.

The Varuna,
and Sahadhamma,
and Accuta,
and Anejaka deities;

the Suleyya and Rucira deities
came,
and the Vasavanesi deities also came.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

The Samana,
Mahasamana,
Manusa,
and Manusuttama deities came.

The Khiddapadosika deities
came,
and the Manopadosika deities also came.

Then came
the Hari deities,
and the Lohitavasi deities.

The Paraga,
and Mahaparaga
deities came,
endowed with glory.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

The Sukka,
Karumha,
and Aruna deities
came,
together with the Veghanasa deities.

The Odatagayha
who are the foremost,
deities came,
and the Vicakkhana deities.

The Sadamatta,
Haragaja,
and Missaka deities,
endowed with glory.

Thundering,
the rain-god Pajjunna came,
who rains upon all directions.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

The Khemiya deities,
the Tusita deities,
the Yama deities,
and the Katthaka deities,
who are endowed with glory.

The Lambitaka,
Lamasettha,
and Jotinama deities,
along with the Asava deities came.

The Nimmanarati deities
came,
and then came
the Paranimmita deities.

These ten groups of tenfold deities,
all
of diverse hues,

Endowed with psychic powers,
luminous,
beautiful in appearance,
and possessed of glory,

Rejoicing,
they approached
of the monks
the assembly
in the great forest.

These sixty groups of deities,
all
of diverse hues,

according to their names and lineage,
came,
and other similar deities
also came along with them.

those who have ended rebirth,
free from defilements,
the taintless ones who have crossed the flood,

saying, 'Let us see
the one who has crossed the flood,
the Faultless One,
who has passed beyond the dark clouds of craving like the moon.'

Subrahma
and Paramatta Brahmas,
who are sons of the Buddha,
came together with their retinue of mighty Brahmas.

Sanankumara,
and the Great Brahma Tissa,
they too came
to the assembly
in the great forest.

A thousand Brahmas from the Brahma realms came,
and among them a Great Brahma outshines the rest.

Born in the Brahma world,
he is endowed with glorious light,
possessing a massive body,
and great glory.

Ten sovereign Brahmas among them
came,
each wielding independent power over their retinues.

And from their midst
came
the Brahma Harita,
surrounded by his retinue.

When all these deities had approached,
the devas along with Inda and the Brahmas,

the army of Mara
approached.
Behold the foolishness of the Dark One (Mara)!

Saying, 'Come!
Seize!
Bind!
With the snare of lust,
let them be bound by you!

All around,
surround them!
Let no one among them escape you!'

Thus,
there in the midst of the assembly,
the great commander Mara
directed his dark army,
sending them forth,
striking the earth with his palm,
and making a terrifying roar.

Just as
a rainy season's
cloud,
thundering
and flashing with lightning,
then,
he (Mara)
retreated,
enraged,
but utterly powerless to bring them under his control.

And having completely understood all that,
having known it well,
having properly assessed the situation,
the All-Seeing Buddha,
then
addressed
the Teacher,
his disciples
who delight in the Dispensation:
'The army of Mara
has approached;
be aware of them,
O monks!'

And those monks,
exerted great effort,
having heard
the instruction of the Buddha.
Mara's army retreated from those who are free from lust;
of those Arahants,
not even a single hair
could they cause to tremble.

All of them,
victorious in the battle against Mara,
having completely transcended fear,
endowed with glory,
rejoice
together with the noble beings,
those disciples
who are renowned among the people.
