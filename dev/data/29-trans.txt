The Discourse on the Analysis of the Truths.

Thus
by me
was heard.
At one time
the Blessed One

at Varanasi
was dwelling,
at Isipatana,
in the Deer Park.

There, indeed,
the Blessed One
the monks
addressed:
"Monks."

"Venerable sir,"
those monks
to the Blessed One
replied.
The Blessed One
said this:

By the Tathagata,
monks,
the Worthy One,
the Fully Enlightened One,

at Varanasi,
at Isipatana,
in the Deer Park,
the unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma
was set in motion,

which cannot be turned back
by any ascetic,
or brahmin,
or deity,
or Mara,
or Brahma,
or by anyone in the world;

that is,
of the Four Noble Truths
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Of the noble truth of suffering,

the making plain.

the announcing,
the teaching,

the analysing,
the making plain.

Of the noble truth of the cessation of suffering,
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,

Of the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering,

By the Tathagata,
monks,
the Worthy One,
the Fully Enlightened One,

at Varanasi,
at Isipatana,
in the Deer Park,
the unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma
was set in motion,

which cannot be turned back
by any ascetic,
or brahmin,
or deity,
or Mara,
or Brahma,
or by anyone in the world;

that is,
of these Four Noble Truths
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Cultivate,
monks,
Sariputta and Moggallana.
Associate with,
monks,
Sariputta and Moggallana.

They are wise monks,
helpers
of their companions in the holy life.

Just like,
monks,
a mother who gives birth,
so is Sariputta.

Just like
of one who has been born
the nurse,
so indeed is Moggallana.

Sariputta,
monks,
in the fruit of stream-entry
trains others;
Moggallana,
in the supreme goal.

Sariputta,
monks,
is able,
the Four Noble Truths
in detail

to announce,
to teach,
to lay down,
to establish,
to open up,
to analyse,
and to make plain.

This the Blessed One said.
Having said this,
the Sublime One
rose from his seat
and into his dwelling
entered.

There, indeed,
the venerable Sariputta,
soon after the Blessed One had left,
addressed the monks:
"Friends, monks."

"Friend,"
those monks
to the venerable Sariputta
replied.
The venerable Sariputta
said this:

By the Tathagata,
friends,
the Worthy One,
the Fully Enlightened One,

at Varanasi,
at Isipatana,
in the Deer Park,
the unsurpassed Wheel of Dhamma
was set in motion,

which cannot be turned back
by any ascetic,
or brahmin,
or deity,
or Mara,
or Brahma,
or by anyone in the world;

that is,
of the Four Noble Truths
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Of which four?
Of the noble truth of suffering,
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Of the noble truth of the origin of suffering,
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Of the noble truth of the cessation of suffering,
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

Of the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering,
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

And what, friends,
is the noble truth of suffering?

Birth is suffering,
ageing is suffering,
death is suffering,
sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair are suffering,

what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering;
in brief,
the five aggregates subject to clinging are suffering.

And what, friends,
is birth?

Whatever, of the various beings,
in the various orders of beings,
is birth,
coming to birth,
descent into a womb,
production,

the manifestation of the aggregates,
the obtaining of the sense bases:
this, friends, is called birth.

And what, friends,
is ageing?

Whatever, of the various beings,
in the various orders of beings,
is ageing,
decrepitude,
broken teeth,
grey hair,
wrinkled skin,

the dwindling of life,
the decay of the faculties:
this, friends, is called ageing.

And what, friends,
is death?

Whatever, of the various beings,
from the various orders of beings,
is the passing away,
the falling away,
the breaking up,
the disappearance,

the dying, the death,
the completion of one's time,
the breaking up of the aggregates,
the laying down of the body,
the cutting off of the life faculty:
this, friends, is called death.

And what, friends,
is sorrow?

Whatever, friends,
with one misfortune or another
for one who is afflicted,
by one painful thing or another
for one who is touched,

is the sorrow,
the sorrowing,
the state of sorrowing,
the inner sorrow,
the inner deep sorrow:
this, friends, is called sorrow.

And what, friends,
is lamentation?

Whatever, friends,
with one misfortune or another
for one who is afflicted,
by one painful thing or another
for one who is touched,

is the wail,
the lament,
the wailing,
the lamenting,
the state of wailing,
the state of lamenting:
this, friends, is called lamentation.

And what, friends,
is pain?

Whatever, friends,
is bodily pain,
bodily discomfort,
born of bodily contact,
what is felt as painful and disagreeable:
this, friends, is called pain.

And what, friends,
is grief?

Whatever, friends,
is mental pain,
mental discomfort that is felt,
born of mental contact,
what is felt as painful and disagreeable:
this, friends, is called grief.

And what, friends,
is despair?

Whatever, friends,
with one misfortune or another
for one who is afflicted,
by one painful thing or another
for one who is touched,

is the trouble,
the despair,
the state of being troubled,
the state of despairing:
this, friends, is called despair.

And what, friends, is meant by
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering?

For beings subject to birth,
friends,
to such beings
this wish
arises:

Oh, if only
we
were not subject to birth,
and truly, to us
birth would not come!

But this
by wishing
cannot be attained.

This too is
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering.

For beings subject to ageing,
friends,
to such beings
this wish
arises:

Oh, if only
we
were not subject to ageing,
and truly, to us
ageing would not come!

But this
by wishing
cannot be attained.

This too is
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering.

For beings subject to sickness,
friends,
to such beings
this wish
arises:

Oh, if only
we
were not subject to sickness,
and truly, to us
sickness would not come!

But this
by wishing
cannot be attained.

This too is
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering.

For beings subject to death,
friends,
to such beings
this wish
arises:

Oh, if only
we
were not subject to death,
and truly, to us
death would not come!

But this
by wishing
cannot be attained.

This too is
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering.

For beings subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair,
friends,
to such beings
this wish
arises:

Oh, if only
we
were not subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair,
and truly, to us
sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair would not come!

But this
by wishing
cannot be attained.

This too is
what one wants
and does not get,
that too is suffering.

And what, friends,
in brief,
are the five aggregates subject to clinging that are suffering?

They are these:
the aggregate of form subject to clinging,
the aggregate of feeling subject to clinging,
the aggregate of perception subject to clinging,
the aggregate of formations subject to clinging,
the aggregate of consciousness subject to clinging.

These, friends, are called,
in brief,
the five aggregates subject to clinging that are suffering.
This, friends, is called
the noble truth of suffering.

And what, friends,
is the noble truth of the origin of suffering?

It is this craving
that leads to renewed existence,
accompanied by delight and lust,
delighting now here, now there.

That is to say:
craving for sensual pleasures,
craving for existence,
craving for non-existence.
This, friends, is called
the noble truth of the origin of suffering.

And what, friends,
is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering?

It is, of that very craving,
the remainderless fading away and cessation,
the giving up,
the relinquishing,
the release,
the non-attachment.

This, friends, is called
the noble truth of the cessation of suffering.

And what, friends,
is the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering?

It is just this
noble
eightfold
path.
That is to say:

right view,
right intention,
right speech,
right action,

right livelihood,
right effort,
right mindfulness,
right concentration.

And what, friends,
is right view?

Whatever, friends,
is knowledge of suffering,
knowledge of the origin of suffering,
knowledge of the cessation of suffering,
knowledge of the way leading to the cessation of suffering:
this, friends, is called right view.

And what, friends,
is right intention?

The intention of renunciation,
the intention of non-ill-will,
the intention of harmlessness:
this, friends, is called right intention.

And what, friends,
is right speech?

Abstaining from false speech,
abstaining from divisive speech,
abstaining from harsh speech,
abstaining from idle chatter:
this, friends, is called right speech.

And what, friends,
is right action?

Abstaining from killing living beings,
abstaining from taking what is not given,
abstaining from sexual misconduct:
this, friends, is called right action.

And what, friends,
is right livelihood?

Here, friends,
a noble disciple,
having abandoned wrong livelihood,
by right livelihood
makes his living:
this, friends, is called right livelihood.

And what, friends,
is right effort?

Here, friends, a monk,
of unarisen
evil
unwholesome states
for the non-arising,

he generates desire,
he strives,
he arouses energy,
he exerts his mind,
he endeavours.

Of arisen
evil
unwholesome states
for the abandoning,

he generates desire,
he strives,
he arouses energy,
he exerts his mind,
he endeavours.

Of unarisen
wholesome states
for the arising,

he generates desire,
he strives,
he arouses energy,
he exerts his mind,
he endeavours.

Of arisen
wholesome states
for the continuance,
the non-disappearance,
the increase,
the abundance,
the fulfilment by development,

he generates desire,
he strives,
he arouses energy,
he exerts his mind,
he endeavours.
This, friends, is called right effort.

And what, friends,
is right mindfulness?

Here, friends, a monk
in the body
contemplating the body
dwells,

ardent,
clearly comprehending,
mindful,
having removed
with regard to the world
covetousness and grief.

In feelings
contemplating feelings
he dwells,

ardent,
clearly comprehending,
mindful,
having removed
with regard to the world
covetousness and grief.

In the mind
contemplating the mind
he dwells,

ardent,
clearly comprehending,
mindful,
having removed
with regard to the world
covetousness and grief.

In mental objects
contemplating mental objects
he dwells,

ardent,
clearly comprehending,
mindful,
having removed
with regard to the world
covetousness and grief.
This, friends, is called right mindfulness.

And what, friends,
is right concentration?

Here, friends, a monk,
quite secluded from sensual pleasures,
secluded from unwholesome states,

accompanied by applied thought,
and sustained thought,
born of seclusion,
with rapture and happiness,
the first jhana
having entered upon,
he dwells.

With the stilling of applied and sustained thought,
with inner confidence,
and unification of mind,

without applied thought,
without sustained thought,
born of concentration,
with rapture and happiness,
the second jhana
having entered upon,
he dwells.

With the fading away of rapture as well,
he dwells in equanimity,
mindful and clearly comprehending,
and he experiences happiness with the body.

That of which the noble ones declare:
'Equanimous,
mindful,
he dwells happily',
that third jhana
having entered upon,
he dwells.

With the abandoning of pleasure
and the abandoning of pain,
and with the earlier
of joy and grief
disappearance,

neither painful nor pleasant,
with purity of mindfulness due to equanimity,
the fourth jhana
having entered upon,
he dwells.

This, friends, is called right concentration.
This, friends, is called
the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering.

By the Tathagata, friends,
the Worthy One,
the Fully Enlightened One,

at Varanasi,

which cannot be turned back
by any ascetic,
or brahmin,
or deity,
or Mara,
or Brahma,
or by anyone in the world;

that is,
of these Four Noble Truths
the announcing,
the teaching,
the laying down,
the establishing,
the opening up,
the analysing,
the making plain.

This the venerable Sariputta said.

those monks

words
