The Discourse on the Ten Dhammas

Thus have I heard:
On one occasion
the Blessed One
in Savatthi
was dwelling
at Jeta's Grove,
in Anathapindika's
monastery.

There,
the Blessed One
addressed the monks,
saying, 'Monks!'
'Venerable sir,' 
those monks
to the Blessed One
replied.
The Blessed One
said this:

These ten
monks,
things
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.
What are the ten?

'To a different appearance (a casteless state)
I have arrived,'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'Dependent on others
is my livelihood,'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'Different
is the
behavior
I must adopt,'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'I wonder if
my own mind
with regard to virtue
does not reproach me?'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'I wonder if
me,
having investigated,
wise fellow monastics
with regard to virtue
do not reproach?'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'From all
that is my
dear
and appealing,
there will be alteration
and separation,'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'I am the owner of my actions,
heir to my actions,
born of my actions,
related to my actions,
and have my actions as my refuge.
Whatever action
I shall do,
whether good
or evil,
of that I will become the heir,'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'For me, being in this state,
how do the days and nights
pass by?'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'I wonder if I
in empty places
take delight?'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

'Is there
in me
any superhuman state,
any fully noble distinction in knowledge and vision attained,
so that I,
in my final days,
by fellow monastics
when questioned,
will not be dismayed?'
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

These
monks,
ten things
by one who has gone forth
constantly
should be reflected upon.

This was said
by the Blessed One.
Elated in mind,
those monks
in the Blessed One's
words
rejoiced.
