The Discourse to Āḷavaka

Thus have I heard.
On one occasion, the Blessed One

was dwelling at Āḷavī,
in the abode of the yakkha Āḷavaka.

Then the yakkha Āḷavaka
approached the Blessed One. Having approached,
he said this to the Blessed One:

"Get out, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went out.
"Come in, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went in.

For a second time, the yakkha Āḷavaka
said this to the Blessed One:

"Get out, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went out.
"Come in, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went in.

For a third time, the yakkha Āḷavaka said this to the Blessed One:

"Get out, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went out.
"Come in, ascetic!"
"Very well, friend," said the Blessed One, and he went in.

For a fourth time, the yakkha Āḷavaka said this to the Blessed One:

"Get out, ascetic!"
"I will not go out, friend."
"Whatever you have to do,"
"do it."

"Ascetic, a question to you
I will ask.
If you do not answer me,
I will overthrow your mind,
or split your heart,
or, grabbing you by the feet, I will hurl you across the Ganges."

"Friend, I do not see anyone capable of doing that."

"Nevertheless, friend,"
"ask whatever you desire."
Then the yakkha Āḷavaka
addressed the Blessed One in a stanza:

"What here in this world
is a person's best wealth?
What, when well practiced,
brings happiness?
What indeed is
the sweetest of tastes?
Living in what manner
do they call life the best?"

"Faith is a person's best wealth here.
The Dhamma, when well practiced,
brings happiness.
Truth is indeed the sweetest of tastes.
Living with wisdom
is what they call the best life."

"How does one cross the flood?
How does one cross the ocean?
How does one overcome suffering?
How is one purified?"

"By faith one crosses the flood.
By diligence one crosses the ocean.
By energy one overcomes suffering.
By wisdom one is purified."

"How does one gain wisdom?
How does one find wealth?
How does one achieve fame?
How does one bind friends?
Passing from this world to the next,
how does one not grieve after passing away?"

"Having faith in the Arahants (and their Dhamma)

Acting appropriately, being steadfast,
and industrious, one finds wealth.
Through truthfulness, one achieves fame.
By giving, one binds friends.

If one has these four virtues,
for a faithful householder:
truthfulness, righteousness,
courage, and generosity,
he indeed does not grieve after passing away.
Passing from this world to the next,
thus he does not grieve after passing away."

"Come now, ask others as well"

"Why should I now ask (others)?"

"Indeed, it was for my benefit that the Buddha came to dwell at Āḷavī.
Today I know where a gift given bears great fruit.

I will wander"

to the Blessed One
he said this:
"Excellent,
O Gotama,
Excellent, O Gotama."

"Just as if,
O Gotama,
what was overturned
were set upright,
or what was hidden
were revealed,
to one who is lost
the path
were pointed out,

in the darkness
or were to hold an oil lamp,
thinking, 'those with eyes will see forms';
in the exact same way,
by the Master Gotama,
in many ways,
the Dhamma has been made clear.

I, myself,
to the Master Gotama
go for refuge,
and to the Dhamma,
and to the Sangha of monks.
as a lay follower.
me
May the Master Gotama
remember.
From today onwards,
for as long as life lasts,
as one who has gone for refuge."
