The Discourse to Aggika Bhāradvāja

Thus have I heard:
On one occasion
the Blessed One
was dwelling at Sāvatthi,
in Jeta's Grove,
in Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery.

Then the Blessed One,
in the morning,
having dressed,
taking his bowl and robe,
into Sāvatthi
entered for alms.

Now at that time,
of the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja,
in the house
a fire was burning
and the oblation was held ready.

Then the Blessed One,
in Sāvatthi,
from house to house in order,
walking for alms,
to where the house of the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja was,
there he approached.

He saw,
the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja,
the Blessed One
coming from afar.
Having seen him,
he said this to the Blessed One:

"Stop right there, shaveling!
Right there, wretched ascetic!
Stay right there, outcast!"

When this was said,
the Blessed One
to the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja
said this:
"But do you know, brahmin,
what an outcast is,
or the things that make one an outcast?"

"No, Master Gotama, I do not know
what an outcast is,
or the things that make one an outcast.

It would be good if Master Gotama, for me,
would teach the Dhamma in such a way
that I may come to know
what an outcast is,
or the things that make one an outcast."

"Then, brahmin,
listen,
pay careful attention;
I shall speak."

"Yes, sir,"
the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja
replied to the Blessed One.
The Blessed One said this:

"Whoever is angry and bears a grudge,
the man who is evil and disparages others' virtues,
who holds wrong views and is deceitful,
know him as an outcast.

Whether once-born or twice-born,
whoever here harms living beings,
who has no compassion for living creatures,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever destroys and besieges
villages and market towns,
and is notorious as an oppressor,
know him as an outcast.

Whether in a village or in the forest,
whatever is cherished by others as their own,
whoever takes by theft what is not given,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, having truly taken on a debt,
runs away when pressed to repay,
saying, 'I owe you no debt,'
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, out of desire for some trifle,
a person travelling along the road
kills, and takes that trifle,
know him as an outcast.

For his own sake or for another's sake,
or for the sake of wealth, the man who,
when questioned as a witness, speaks falsely,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, with relatives' or friends'
wives is seen misbehaving,
whether by force or by mutual consent,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever his mother or father,
aged and past their youth,
though being able, does not support,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever his mother or father,
brother, sister or mother-in-law
strikes, or angers with words,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, when asked about what is beneficial,
teaches what is harmful,
and gives counsel in a concealed way,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, having done an evil deed,
wishes, 'May no one know me,'
whoever is a doer of hidden deeds,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, having gone to another's home
and eaten fine food there,
does not honour that host in return when he comes,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever a brahmin or an ascetic,
or any other mendicant,
deceives with false speech,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever a brahmin or an ascetic,
when mealtime has arrived,
angers with words and gives nothing,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever here speaks what is untrue,
wrapped up in delusion,
grasping after some trifle,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever exalts himself
and despises others,
debased by his own conceit,
know him as an outcast.

One who provokes anger and is stingy,
of evil wishes, miserly and crafty,
shameless and without fear of wrongdoing,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever reviles the Buddha,
or a disciple of his,
whether a wanderer or a householder,
know him as an outcast.

Whoever, though not being an arahant,
claims to be an arahant,
is a thief in the world together with its Brahmās;
he indeed is the lowest of outcasts.
These indeed are called outcasts,
those whom I have made known to you.

Not by birth is one an outcast,
not by birth is one a brahmin.
By deed one becomes an outcast,
by deed one becomes a brahmin.

Know it also by this,
as by this example of mine:
there was an outcast's son, Sopāka,
renowned as Mātaṅga.

He attained the highest fame,
this Mātaṅga, fame so very hard to gain.
There came to attend upon him
many nobles and brahmins.

He mounted the divine vehicle,
the stainless great highway;
having discarded sensual passion,
he became one reborn in the Brahma world.

Birth did not prevent him
from rebirth in the Brahma world.
Those born into a family of Vedic reciters,
brahmins who are kinsmen of the sacred hymns,

even they, in evil deeds,
are repeatedly seen to be involved.
In this very life they are blameworthy,
and in the next life there is a bad destination.
Birth does not protect them
from a bad destination or from blame.

Not by birth is one an outcast,
not by birth is one a brahmin.
By deed one becomes an outcast,
by deed one becomes a brahmin."

When this was said,
the brahmin Aggika Bhāradvāja
said this to the Blessed One:

"Excellent, Master Gotama!
Excellent, Master Gotama!
Just as, Master Gotama,
one might set upright what had been overturned,
or reveal what was hidden,
or show the way to one who was lost,
or hold up an oil lamp in the darkness
so that those with eyes might see forms,

in the same way, by Master Gotama,
in many ways,
the Dhamma has been made clear.

I
to Master Gotama
go for refuge,
and to the Dhamma and to the Saṅgha of monks.

As a lay follower, me
may Master Gotama remember,
from this day forth,
for as long as life lasts,
as one who has gone for refuge."
