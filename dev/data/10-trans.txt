The Discourse on Loving-Kindness.

This is what should be done by one skilled in good,
who wishes to
attain the state of peace,
having comprehended it:

One must be able,
upright,
thoroughly upright,
amenable to instruction,
gentle,
and not arrogant.

Contented,
easily supported,
with few duties,
and living lightly,

with peaceful senses,
wise,
courteous,
and not greedily attached to families.

One should not commit even the slightest wrong
for which
other wise people
might reprove.

May they be happy
and secure.
May all beings
be happy at heart.

Whatever
living beings there may be,
whether they are feeble
or strong,
without exception,

whether long,
those who are
large,
medium,
short, fine, or gross,

whether seen
or unseen,
those dwelling far
or near,

those who are born
or seeking birth:
may all beings
be
happy at heart.

Let not
one
another
deceive,
nor despise
anywhere,
anyone at all.

Out of physical or verbal anger
or thoughts of ill-will,
to each other
one should not wish suffering.

Just as a mother
would
her own child,
with her life,
protect her only child,

so too
towards all beings
a mind of loving-kindness
one should cultivate,
that is boundless.

With loving-kindness
towards the entire world,
one should cultivate a mind
that is boundless,

above,
below,
and across,
unobstructed,
free from hatred,
and free from enmity.

Whether standing,
walking,
seated,
or lying down,
as long as one is awake,

this mindfulness
one should resolve upon.
This is the divine abiding,
thus it is said here.

False views
not falling into,
being virtuous
and endowed with insight,

in sensual pleasures
having removed greed,
surely he does not
to lie in a womb
come again.
