The Discourse on the Crest of the Banner

Thus
by me
have I heard:
On one occasion,
the Blessed One
near Savatthi,
was dwelling
in Jeta's Grove,
Anathapindika's
monastery.

There, indeed,
the Blessed One
the monks
addressed:
"Monks!"
"Venerable Sir,"
those monks
to the Blessed One
replied.
The Blessed One
said this:

In the past,
Monks,
a battle between gods and asuras
took place.
Then, indeed,
Monks,
Sakka, the lord of the gods,
the gods of Tavatimsa
addressed:

If,
dear sirs,
for the gods
who have gone to battle,
there should arise
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
at that time, to my own
crest of the banner
you should look up.

For of you, looking at my
crest of the banner,
looking up,
whatever will be
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

If you do not look up at the crest of my banner,
then
of Pajapati, the deity king's
crest of the banner
you should look up.

For of you, looking at Pajapati the deity king's
crest of the banner,
looking up,
whatever will be
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

If you do not look up at the crest of the banner of Pajapati, the deity king,
then
of Varuna, the deity king's
crest of the banner
you should look up.

For of you, looking at Varuna the deity king's
crest of the banner,
looking up,
whatever will be
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

If you do not look up at the crest of the banner of Varuna, the deity king,
then
of Isana, the deity king's
crest of the banner
you should look up.

For of you, looking at Isana the deity king's
crest of the banner,
looking up,
whatever will be
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

But then,
Monks,
of Sakka, the lord of the gods,
the crest of the banner
for those looking up at,
or of Pajapati, the deity king,
the crest of the banner
for those looking up at,
or of Varuna, the deity king,
the crest of the banner
for those looking up at,
or of Isana, the deity king,
the crest of the banner
for those looking up at,
whatever fear that will arise, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
may be abandoned,
or may not be abandoned.
What is the reason for that?

Monks, Sakka, the lord of the gods,
is not free from lust,
is not free from hatred,
is not free from delusion;
he is subject to fear,
subject to terror,
subject to fright,
and subject to fleeing.

But I, indeed,
Monks,
to you
say this:
If,
to you,
Monks,
who have gone to a forest,
to the foot of a tree,
or to an empty place,
there should arise
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation,
me alone
at that time
you should recollect:

Thus indeed:
the Blessed One is
accomplished,
fully awakened,
endowed with clear vision and virtuous conduct,
sublime,
the knower of worlds,
incomparable,
the leader of persons to be tamed,
the teacher of gods and humans,
awakened,
and blessed.

Monks, for you recollecting me,
whatever fear there will be, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

If you do not recollect me,
then
the Dhamma
you should recollect:

Well-expounded
by the Blessed One
is the Dhamma,
visible here and now,
immediately effective,
inviting inspection,
leading onwards,
personally
to be realized
by the wise.

Monks, for you recollecting the Dhamma,
whatever fear there will be, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.

If you do not recollect the Dhamma,
then
the Sangha
you should recollect:

The Sangha of the Blessed One's disciples is practicing the good way,
the Sangha of the Blessed One's disciples is practicing the straight way,
the Sangha of the Blessed One's disciples is practicing the true way,
the Sangha of the Blessed One's disciples is practicing the proper way;

that is,
the four pairs of persons,
the eight kinds of individuals,
this Sangha of the Blessed One's disciples
is worthy of offerings,
worthy of hospitality,
worthy of gifts,
worthy of reverential salutation,
the incomparable
field of merit
for the world.

Monks, for you recollecting the Sangha,
whatever fear there will be, or
terror, or
horripilation,
that
will be abandoned.
What is the reason for that?

The Tathagata,
Monks,
being accomplished
and fully awakened,
is free from lust,
is free from hatred,
is free from delusion;
he is not subject to fear,
not subject to terror,
not subject to fright,
and not subject to fleeing.

This was said
by the Blessed One.
Having said this,
the Sublime One,
further
said this:
the Teacher:

In the forest,
at the foot of a tree,
or in an empty place,
Monks,
you should recollect
the Fully Awakened One,
and fear
for you
will not exist.

If you do not recollect the Buddha,
the foremost in the world,
the chief of men,
then
the Dhamma
you should recollect,
the leading out,
well-expounded.

If you do not recollect the Dhamma,
the leading out,
well-expounded,
then
the Sangha
you should recollect,
the field of merit,
incomparable.

Thus,
the Buddha,
for those recollecting
the Dhamma,
and the Sangha,
Monks,
fear, or
terror, or
horripilation
will not occur.
