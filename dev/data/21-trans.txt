The Girimananda Sutta (The Discourse to Girimananda).

Thus
by me
was heard:
On one occasion
the Blessed One

near Savatthi
was dwelling
in Jeta's Grove,
Anathapindika's
monastery.

Now at that time
Venerable Girimananda
was afflicted,
suffering,
and gravely ill.

Then
Venerable Ananda
where
the Blessed One was,
there
approached;
having approached,

the Blessed One
having paid homage to,
to one side
he sat down.

To one side
seated,
Venerable Ananda
to the Blessed One
said this:

Venerable
Sir,
Girimananda
is afflicted,
suffering,
and gravely ill.

It would be good,
Venerable Sir,
if the Blessed One,
where Venerable Girimananda is,
there
would approach,
out of compassion.

If indeed,
Ananda, you
to the monk Girimananda
having approached,
the ten perceptions
would recite,

it is indeed possible
that it occurs,
that
for the monk Girimananda,

having heard the ten perceptions,
that illness
immediately
would subside.
What are the ten?

The perception of impermanence,
the perception of non-self,
the perception of unattractiveness,
the perception of danger,

the perception of abandoning,
the perception of dispassion,
the perception of cessation,
the perception of distaste for every world,

the perception of impermanence in all fabrications,
and mindfulness of breathing.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of impermanence?

Here, Ananda,
a monk,
gone to a forest,
or gone to the root of a tree,
or gone to an empty hut,
reflects thus:

Form is impermanent,
feeling is impermanent,
perception is impermanent,
fabrications are impermanent,
consciousness is impermanent.

Thus
in these five aggregates subject to clinging
he dwells contemplating impermanence.
This, Ananda, is called the perception of impermanence.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of non-self?

Here, Ananda,
a monk,
gone to a forest,
or gone to the root of a tree,
or gone to an empty hut,
thus
reflects:

The eye is non-self;
forms are non-self;
the ear is non-self;
sounds are non-self;

The nose is non-self;
odors are non-self;
the tongue is non-self;
tastes are non-self;

The body is non-self;
tactile sensations are non-self;
the mind is non-self;
mental phenomena are non-self.
Thus.

Thus
in these six internal and external sense bases,
he dwells contemplating non-self.
This, Ananda, is called the perception of non-self.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of unattractiveness?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
reviews this very body,
upward from the soles of the feet,

downward from the crown of the head,
enveloped by the skin,
and full of various kinds of impurities,
reflecting:

There are
in this body:
head hairs,
body hairs,
nails,
teeth,
skin,

flesh,
sinews,
bones,
bone marrow,
kidneys,
heart,
liver,
pleura,
spleen,
lungs,

intestines,
mesentery,
undigested food,
feces,
bile,
phlegm,
pus,
blood,
sweat,
fat,

tears,
grease,
saliva,
mucus,
synovial fluid,
and urine.

Thus
in this body
contemplating unattractiveness
he dwells.
This, Ananda, is called the perception of unattractiveness.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of danger?

Here, Ananda,
a monk,
gone to a forest,
or gone to the root of a tree,
or gone to an empty hut,
thus
reflects:

This body has much suffering
and much danger.

Thus
in this body
various illnesses
arise,
namely:

eye-disease,
ear-disease,
nose-disease,
tongue-disease,
body-disease,
head-disease,

disease of the external ear,
mouth-disease,
tooth-disease,

cough,
asthma,
catarrh,
burning fever,
fever,
stomach-ache,

fainting,
dysentery,
colic / shooting pain,
cholera,
leprosy,
boils,
ringworm,
consumption,
epilepsy,

skin eruption,
itch,
scab,
scratching disease / numbness,
scabies,
hemorrhaging / bile-blood,
diabetes,

hemiplegia / paralysis,
cancer,
fistula,
illnesses arising from bile,

illnesses arising from phlegm,
illnesses arising from wind,

illnesses arising from the conflict of the humors,
illnesses arising from changes in weather,

illnesses arising from improper care,
illnesses arising from assault,
illnesses arising from the result of kamma,

cold,
heat,
hunger,
thirst,
excrement,
and urine.

Thus
in this body
contemplating danger
he dwells.
This, Ananda, is called the perception of danger.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of abandoning?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
an arisen sensual thought
does not tolerate,
abandons it,
dispels it,

destroys it,
and brings it to non-existence.

an arisen thought of ill-will
does not tolerate,
abandons it,
dispels it,

destroys it,
and brings it to non-existence.

an arisen thought of cruelty
does not tolerate,
abandons it,
dispels it,
destroys it,
and brings it to non-existence.

whatever arisen
evil, unwholesome states
he does not tolerate,
abandons them,
dispels them,
destroys them,
and brings them to non-existence.

This is called, Ananda, the perception of abandoning.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of dispassion?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
having gone to a forest,
or to the foot of a tree,
or to an empty hut,
reflects thus:

'This is peaceful,
this is sublime,
that is to say,
the calming of all formations,

the relinquishing of all attachments,
the destruction of craving,
dispassion,
Nibbana.'

This is called, Ananda, the perception of dispassion.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of cessation?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
having gone to a forest,
or to the foot of a tree,
or to an empty hut,
reflects thus:

'This is peaceful,
this is sublime,
that is to say,
the calming of all formations,

the relinquishing of all attachments,
the destruction of craving,
cessation,
Nibbana.'

This is called, Ananda, the perception of cessation.

And what,
Ananda,
is the perception of non-delight in the entire world?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
whatever engagements and clingings there are in the world,

mental
standpoints and adherences,
and underlying tendencies,
abandoning them,
he abstains and does not cling.

This is called, Ananda, the perception of non-delight in the entire world.

And what,
Ananda,
in all formations
is the perception of impermanence?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
by all formations
is troubled,
is ashamed,
and is disgusted.

This is called, Ananda, the perception of impermanence in all formations.

And what,
Ananda,
is mindfulness of breathing?

Here, Ananda,
a monk
having gone to a forest,
or to the foot of a tree,
or to an empty hut,
sits down,
having folded his legs crosswise,

keeping his body erect,
and setting mindfulness to the forefront.

He
mindfully
breathes in;
mindfully
breathes out.

Long
while breathing in,
he discerns: 'I breathe in long.'

Or long while breathing out,
he discerns: 'I breathe out long.'

Short while breathing in,
he discerns: 'I breathe in short.'

Or short while breathing out,
he discerns: 'I breathe out short.'

'Experiencing the whole body,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing the whole body,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Tranquilizing the bodily formation,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Tranquilizing the bodily formation,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing rapture,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing rapture,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing bliss,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing bliss,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing the mental formation,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing the mental formation,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Tranquilizing the mental formation,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Tranquilizing the mental formation,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing the mind,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Experiencing the mind,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Gladdening the mind,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Gladdening the mind,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Concentrating the mind,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Concentrating the mind,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Liberating the mind,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Liberating the mind,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating impermanence,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating impermanence,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating fading away,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating fading away,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating cessation,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating cessation,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating relinquishment,
I will breathe in,'
he trains himself.

'Contemplating relinquishment,
I will breathe out,'
he trains himself.

This is called, Ananda, mindfulness of breathing.

If indeed,
you, Ananda,
to the monk Girimananda
having approached,
these ten perceptions
were to recite,

it is indeed possible
that this case exists:
that
for the monk Girimananda

having heard these ten perceptions,
that affliction
immediately
would subside.

Then indeed,
Venerable Ananda,
in the presence of the Blessed One,
these ten perceptions
having learned,

where Venerable Girimananda was,
there
he approached;
and having approached,

to Venerable Girimananda
these ten perceptions
he recited.

Then indeed,
for Venerable Girimananda,
having heard these ten perceptions,
that affliction
immediately
subsided.

Rose up (recovered)
Venerable Girimananda did
from that affliction.

And thus that affliction of Venerable Girimananda was abandoned.
