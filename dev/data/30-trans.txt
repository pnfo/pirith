The Āṭānāṭiya Discourse

Thus have I heard:
On one occasion
the Blessed One
near Rājagaha
was dwelling
on Vulture Peak mountain.

Then
the Four Great Kings,
with a great army of yakkhas,
with a great army of gandhabbas,
with a great army of kumbhaṇḍas,
and with a great army of nāgas,

having set a guard over the four quarters,
having stationed troops over the four quarters,
having posted a barrier over the four quarters,

when the night was far advanced,
of surpassing beauty,
the whole of Vulture Peak
illuminating,
to where the Blessed One was
they approached.

Having approached,
they bowed to the Blessed One
and sat down to one side.

And of those yakkhas,
some
bowed to the Blessed One
and sat down to one side.

Some
with the Blessed One
exchanged greetings,
and after pleasant talk,
courteous and memorable,
had been concluded,
sat down to one side.

Some,
towards where the Blessed One was
raising their joined palms,
sat down to one side.

Some,
announcing their name and clan,
sat down to one side.
Some,
remaining silent,
sat down to one side.

Seated to one side,
the great king Vessavaṇa
to the Blessed One
said this:

There are, venerable sir,
eminent yakkhas
who have no faith in the Blessed One.
There are, venerable sir,
eminent yakkhas
who have faith in the Blessed One.

There are, venerable sir,
middling yakkhas
who have no faith in the Blessed One.
There are, venerable sir,
middling yakkhas
who have faith in the Blessed One.

There are, venerable sir,
lowly yakkhas
who have no faith in the Blessed One.
There are, venerable sir,
lowly yakkhas
who have faith in the Blessed One.

But for the most part, venerable sir,
the yakkhas
are indeed without faith
in the Blessed One.
What is the reason for that?

Because the Blessed One, venerable sir,
for abstaining from killing living beings
teaches the Dhamma;
for abstaining from taking what is not given
he teaches the Dhamma;

for abstaining from sexual misconduct
he teaches the Dhamma;
for abstaining from false speech
he teaches the Dhamma;
for abstaining from liquor and intoxicants that cause heedlessness
he teaches the Dhamma.

But for the most part, venerable sir,
the yakkhas
do not abstain from killing living beings,
do not abstain from taking what is not given,
do not abstain from sexual misconduct,
do not abstain from false speech,
do not abstain from liquor and intoxicants that cause heedlessness.

To them that teaching
is unwelcome and displeasing.

There are, venerable sir,
disciples of the Blessed One
who in the wilderness,
deep in the forest,
remote lodgings
resort to,

where there is little sound,
little noise,
far from the bustle of people,
hidden away from humankind,
suitable for seclusion.

There
dwell
eminent yakkhas
as residents,
who
in this teaching of the Blessed One
have no faith.

So that they may gain faith,
may he learn,
venerable sir, the Blessed One,
the Āṭānāṭiya protection,

for the monks,
the nuns,
the laymen
and the laywomen,
for their guarding,
for their protection,
for their freedom from harm,
and for their dwelling in comfort.

Consented
the Blessed One
by remaining silent.

Then
the great king Vessavaṇa,
the Blessed One's consent
having understood,
on that occasion
this Āṭānāṭiya protection
recited:

Homage to Vipassī,
the one with vision,
the glorious.
Homage also to Sikhī,
compassionate to all beings.

Homage to Vessabhū,
the one washed clean,
the ascetic.
Homage to Kakusandha,
crusher of Māra's army.

Homage to Koṇāgamana,
the true brahmin,
who has lived the holy life.
Homage to Kassapa,
freed in every respect.

Homage to Aṅgīrasa,
son of the Sakyans,
the glorious,
who
taught this Dhamma
that dispels all suffering.

And those who
are quenched in the world,
things as they really are
having clearly seen,
those people
are free of slander,
great,
and free from fear.

The benefactor of gods and humans,
Gotama, to whom they pay homage,
perfect in knowledge and conduct,
great,
and free from fear.

Where rises
the sun,
child of Aditi,
the great orb,
at whose rising
the night comes to an end,

and when that sun has risen
it is called "day".
There is also a lake there,
deep,
an ocean
of flowing waters.
So they know it there:
"the ocean of flowing waters".

From here
that is the eastern quarter,
so the people call it,
the quarter that is guarded by
that glorious great king.

Lord of the gandhabbas,
Dhataraṭṭha is his name.
He delights in dance and song,
honoured by the gandhabbas.

He has many sons too,
all of one name, so I have heard:
eighty and ten and one,
Inda by name,
of mighty strength.

They too,
having seen the Buddha,
the Buddha, kinsman of the sun,
pay homage from afar
to the great one, free from fear:
"Homage to you, thoroughbred among men!
Homage to you, supreme among men!

With skilful insight you look upon us;
even the non-humans worship you."
This we have heard again and again,
therefore we speak thus:

"Do you worship Gotama the Conqueror?"
"We worship Gotama the Conqueror;
perfect in knowledge and conduct,
the Buddha Gotama we worship."

Where the departed are said to go,
the slanderers,
the backbiters,
the killers of living beings,
the cruel,
the thieves
and the deceitful people,

From here
that is the southern quarter,
so the people call it,
the quarter that is guarded by
that glorious great king.

Lord of the kumbhaṇḍas,
Virūḷha is his name.
He delights in dance and song,
honoured by the kumbhaṇḍas.

He has many sons too,
all of one name, so I have heard:
eighty and ten and one,
Inda by name,
of mighty strength.

They too,
having seen the Buddha,
the Buddha, kinsman of the sun,
pay homage from afar
to the great one, free from fear:
"Homage to you, thoroughbred among men!
Homage to you, supreme among men!

With skilful insight you look upon us;
even the non-humans worship you."
This we have heard again and again,
therefore we speak thus:

"Do you worship Gotama the Conqueror?"
"We worship Gotama the Conqueror;
perfect in knowledge and conduct,
the Buddha Gotama we worship."

Where sets
the sun,
child of Aditi,
the great orb,
at whose setting
the day comes to an end,

and when that sun has set
it is called "night".
There is also a lake there,
deep,
an ocean
of flowing waters.
So they know it there:
"the ocean of flowing waters".

From here
that is the western quarter,
so the people call it,
the quarter that is guarded by
that glorious great king.

Lord of the nāgas,
Virūpakkha is his name.
He delights in dance and song,
honoured by the nāgas.

He has many sons too,
all of one name, so I have heard:
eighty and ten and one,
Inda by name,
of mighty strength.

They too,
having seen the Buddha,
the Buddha, kinsman of the sun,
pay homage from afar
to the great one, free from fear:
"Homage to you, thoroughbred among men!
Homage to you, supreme among men!

With skilful insight you look upon us;
even the non-humans worship you."
This we have heard again and again,
therefore we speak thus:

"Do you worship Gotama the Conqueror?"
"We worship Gotama the Conqueror;
perfect in knowledge and conduct,
the Buddha Gotama we worship."

Where lies delightful Uttarakuru
and beautiful Mount Neru,
there people are born
without selfishness,
without possessiveness.

They do not sow seed,
nor are ploughs drawn;
rice that ripens on untilled ground
the people eat.

Free of bran and husk,
pure,
fragrant
grains of rice
they cook in a pot,
and then eat their meal.

Making a cow their single-hoofed mount,
they ride about from place to place.
Making a beast their single-hoofed mount,
they ride about from place to place.

Making women their vehicle,
they ride about from place to place.
Making men their vehicle,
they ride about from place to place.

Making girls their vehicle,
they ride about from place to place.
Making boys their vehicle,
they ride about from place to place.

Mounting their vehicles,
they travel around in all directions,
the attendants of that king.
Elephant vehicles,
horse vehicles
and divine vehicles
stand ready.

Palaces and palanquins too
belong to that glorious great king.
And he has cities,
well built in the sky:

Āṭānāṭā,
Kusināṭā,
Parakusināṭā,
Nāṭapuriyā
and Parakusitanāṭā.

To the north is Kapīvanta,
and on the other side Janogha,
Navanavutiya,
Ambara-Ambaravatiya,
and the royal capital named Āḷakamandā.

And the great king Kuvera, dear sir,
has a royal capital named Visāṇā;
therefore the great king Kuvera
is called Vessavaṇa.

Each making his own inquiries, they report to him:
Tatolā,
Tattalā,
Tatotalā,
Ojasi,
Tejasi,
Tatojasi,
Sūra, Rājā,
Ariṭṭha and Nemi.

There is also a lake there
named Dharaṇī,
from which the clouds rain down,
from which the rains spread out.
There is also a hall there
named Bhagalavatī,
where the yakkhas gather in attendance.

There the trees are ever in fruit,
thronged with flocks of many kinds of birds,
resounding with the cries of peacocks and herons
and with the sweet song of cuckoos and others.

Here is the call of the jīvaṃjīvaka birds,
and also the oṭṭhavacittaka birds,
the jungle fowl,
the golden crabs,
and in the wood the pokkharasātaka birds.

Here is the sound of parrots and mynahs,
and the daṇḍamāṇavaka birds too.
It is beautiful at all times,
Kuvera's lotus pond, always.

From here
that is the northern quarter,
so the people call it,
the quarter that is guarded by
that glorious great king.

Lord of the yakkhas,
Kuvera is his name.
He delights in dance and song,
honoured by the yakkhas.

He has many sons too,
all of one name, so I have heard:
eighty and ten and one,
Inda by name,
of mighty strength.

They too,
having seen the Buddha,
the Buddha, kinsman of the sun,
pay homage from afar
to the great one, free from fear:
"Homage to you, thoroughbred among men!
Homage to you, supreme among men!

With skilful insight you look upon us;
even the non-humans worship you."
This we have heard again and again,
therefore we speak thus:

"Do you worship Gotama the Conqueror?"
"We worship Gotama the Conqueror;
perfect in knowledge and conduct,
the Buddha Gotama we worship."

This, dear sir,
is the Āṭānāṭiya protection

for the monks,
the nuns,
the laymen
and the laywomen,
for their guarding,
for their protection,
for their freedom from harm,
and for their dwelling in comfort.

Whoever, dear sir,
whether monk
or nun,
layman
or laywoman,
this Āṭānāṭiya protection
has well learnt
and completely mastered,

if that person
any non-human,
whether a male yakkha
or a female yakkha,
a yakkha boy
or a yakkha girl,
a yakkha chief minister,
a yakkha councillor
or a yakkha attendant;

whether a male gandhabba
or a female gandhabba,
a gandhabba boy
or a gandhabba girl,
a gandhabba chief minister,
a gandhabba councillor
or a gandhabba attendant;

whether a male kumbhaṇḍa
or a female kumbhaṇḍa,
a kumbhaṇḍa boy
or a kumbhaṇḍa girl,
a kumbhaṇḍa chief minister,
a kumbhaṇḍa councillor
or a kumbhaṇḍa attendant;

whether a male nāga
or a female nāga,
a nāga boy
or a nāga girl,
a nāga chief minister,
a nāga councillor
or a nāga attendant;

with a mind of ill will,
a monk
or a nun,
a layman
or a laywoman,

should follow them as they walk,
stand near them as they stand,
sit near them as they sit,
or lie near them as they lie down,

then, dear sir, from me that
non-human
would not receive,
in villages
or in towns,
hospitality
or respect.

Nor, dear sir, from me would that
non-human
receive,
in the royal capital named Āḷakamandā,
a site
or a dwelling.

Nor, dear sir, from me would that
non-human
get leave
to the assembly of the yakkhas
to go.

Moreover, dear sir, him
the non-humans
would bar from taking a bride
and from giving one in marriage.

Moreover, dear sir, him
the non-humans
with personal and thorough
insults
would abuse.

Moreover, dear sir,
the non-humans
an empty bowl
would turn upside down over his head.

Moreover, dear sir,
the non-humans
his head into seven pieces
would split.

For there are, dear sir,
non-humans
who are fierce,
cruel
and violent.

Neither to the Great Kings
do they pay heed,
nor to the Great Kings' men
do they pay heed,
nor to the men of the Great Kings' men
do they pay heed.

These, dear sir,
non-humans
of the Great Kings
are called rebels.

Just as, dear sir,
in the realm of the king of Magadha
there are great bandits:

Neither to the king of Magadha
do they pay heed,
nor to the king of Magadha's men
do they pay heed,
nor to the men of the king of Magadha's men
do they pay heed.

These, dear sir,
great bandits
of the king of Magadha
are called rebels.

Even so, dear sir,
there are
non-humans
who are fierce,
cruel
and violent.

Neither to the Great Kings
do they pay heed,
nor to the Great Kings' men
do they pay heed,
nor to the men of the Great Kings' men
do they pay heed.

These, dear sir,
non-humans
of the Great Kings
are called rebels.

If any, dear sir,
non-human,
whether a male yakkha
or a female yakkha,
a yakkha boy
or a yakkha girl,
a yakkha chief minister,
a yakkha councillor
or a yakkha attendant;

whether a male gandhabba
or a female gandhabba,
a gandhabba boy
or a gandhabba girl,
a gandhabba chief minister,
a gandhabba councillor
or a gandhabba attendant;

whether a male kumbhaṇḍa
or a female kumbhaṇḍa,
a kumbhaṇḍa boy
or a kumbhaṇḍa girl,
a kumbhaṇḍa chief minister,
a kumbhaṇḍa councillor
or a kumbhaṇḍa attendant;

whether a male nāga
or a female nāga,
a nāga boy
or a nāga girl,
a nāga chief minister,
a nāga councillor
or a nāga attendant;

with a mind of ill will,
a monk
or a nun,
a layman
or a laywoman,

should follow them as they walk,
stand near them as they stand,
sit near them as they sit,
or lie near them as they lie down,

To these yakkhas,
great yakkhas,
generals
and great generals,
one should complain,
cry out
and call aloud:

"This yakkha is seizing me,
this yakkha is possessing me,
this yakkha is harassing me,
this yakkha is tormenting me,
this yakkha is hurting me,
this yakkha is injuring me,
this yakkha will not let me go!"

To which yakkhas,
great yakkhas,
generals
and great generals?

Inda, Soma and Varuṇa,
Bhāradvāja, Pajāpati,
Candana and Kāmaseṭṭha,
Kinnughaṇḍu and Nighaṇḍu;

Panāda and Opamañña,
Devasūta and Mātali,
Cittasena the gandhabba,
Naḷa, Rājā, Janesabha;

Sātāgira, Hemavata,
Puṇṇaka, Karatiya, Guḷa,
Sīvaka and Mucalinda,
Vessāmitta, Yugandhara;

Gopāla and Suppagedha,
Hiri, Netti and Mandiya,
Pañcālacaṇḍa, Āḷavaka,
Pajjunna, Sumana,
Sumukha, Dadhimukha,
Maṇi, Māṇi, Cara, Dīgha,
and together with them Serissaka.

To these yakkhas,
great yakkhas,
generals
and great generals,
one should complain,
cry out
and call aloud:

"This yakkha is seizing me,
this yakkha is possessing me,
this yakkha is harassing me,
this yakkha is tormenting me,
this yakkha is hurting me,
this yakkha is injuring me,
this yakkha will not let me go!"

This, dear sir,
is the Āṭānāṭiya protection

for the monks,
the nuns,
the laymen
and the laywomen,
for their guarding,
for their protection,
for their freedom from harm,
and for their dwelling in comfort.

Well now, dear sir, we
must go;
we have many duties,
much to do."

"Great Kings, for whatever you now
think it is the time."

Then
the Four Great Kings
rose from their seats,
bowed to the Blessed One,
circled him keeping him on their right,
and vanished right there.

And those yakkhas too
rose from their seats.
Some
bowed to the Blessed One,
circled him keeping him on their right,
and vanished right there.

Some
with the Blessed One
exchanged greetings,
and after pleasant talk,
courteous and memorable,
had been concluded,
vanished right there.

Some,
towards where the Blessed One was
raising their joined palms,
vanished right there.

Some,
announcing their name and clan,
vanished right there.
Some,
remaining silent,
vanished right there.
