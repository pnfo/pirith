The Discourse to Kasībhāradvāja

Thus have I heard: On one occasion, the Blessed One
was dwelling in Magadha, at Dakkhināgiri, in the brahmin village of Ekanālā.

Now at that time, the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja
had harnessed about five hundred ploughs as it was the sowing season.

Then the Blessed One, having dressed in the morning, took his bowl and outer robe
and approached the workplace of the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja.
At that time, food was being distributed by the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja.

Then the Blessed One went to the place where food was being distributed, and having approached,
stood to one side.
The brahmin Kasībhāradvāja saw the Blessed One standing for alms, and having seen Him, said this to the Blessed One:

"Ascetic, I plough and I sow, and having ploughed and sown, I eat.
You too, ascetic, should plough and sow, and having ploughed and sown, you should eat."
"Brahmin, I too plough and sow, and having ploughed and sown, I eat."

"But we do not see the Master Gotama's yoke, or plough, or ploughshare, or goad, or oxen.
Yet the Master Gotama says: 'Brahmin, I too plough and sow, and having ploughed and sown, I eat.'"
Then the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja addressed the Blessed One in a stanza:

"You claim to be a farmer, but we do not see your ploughing.
Being asked, tell us about your ploughing, so that we may understand your farming."

"Faith is the seed,
austerity is the rain,
and wisdom is my yoke and plough.
Modesty is the plough-pole,
mind is the yoke-tie,
and mindfulness is my ploughshare and goad.

Guarded in body,
guarded in speech,
restrained in eating food,
I make truth my weeding,
and gentleness is my unyoking.

"Energy is my beast of burden, carrying me to the supreme security from bondage (Nibbana). It goes without turning back, to where, having gone, one does not sorrow.

Thus is this ploughing done; it bears the fruit of the Deathless. Having done this ploughing, one is freed from all suffering."
Then the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja, having served milk-rice into a large bronze bowl, offered it to the Blessed One, saying: "Let the Master Gotama eat this milk-rice. The Master is indeed a farmer, since the Master Gotama does the ploughing that bears the fruit of the Deathless."

"Food obtained by reciting verses is not to be eaten by me.
This, brahmin, is not the principle of those who see clearly.
The Buddhas reject food obtained by reciting verses.
As long as the Dhamma exists, brahmin, this is their mode of conduct.

"To a fully perfected great sage, whose taints are destroyed and whose remorse is stilled,
you should offer other food and drink.
For he is the field of merit for one seeking merit."

"Then to whom, Master Gotama,
should I give this milk-rice?"

"Brahmin, I do not see anyone in this world with its devas, māras, and brahmās, in this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its devas and humans, by whom this milk-rice, if eaten, could be properly digested, except by the Tathāgata or a disciple of the Tathāgata."

"Therefore, brahmin, throw this milk-rice away where there is little grass, or drop it into water where there are no living creatures."

Then the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja
dropped that milk-rice into water where there were no living creatures.
Then that milk-rice, thrown into the water, hissed and bubbled, producing a 'cicciṭa' and 'ciṭiciṭa' sound,
steaming and smoking profusely.

Just as a ploughshare, heated all day, when thrown into water
hisses and bubbles,
steaming and smoking profusely,
exactly in the same way, that milk-rice, thrown into the water, hissed and bubbled, steaming and smoking profusely.

Then the brahmin Kasībhāradvāja,
alarmed and with hair standing on end,
approached the Blessed One, and having approached,
fell with his head at the feet of the Blessed One
and said this to the Blessed One:

"Excellent, Master Gotama! Excellent, Master Gotama!
Just as one might set upright what was overturned,
or reveal what was hidden,
or show the way to one who is lost,
or hold up an oil lamp in the dark so that those with eyes might see forms,

in the same way, the Master Gotama
has made the Dhamma clear in various ways.
I go to the Master Gotama for refuge, and to the Dhamma, and to the Sangha of monks.
May I receive the going forth (ordination) in the presence of Master Gotama, may I receive higher ordination."

The brahmin Kasībhāradvāja received in the presence of the Blessed One
the going forth, and he received the higher ordination.
And soon after his higher ordination, the venerable Bhāradvāja,
dwelling alone, secluded,
earnest, ardent, and resolute,
before long, that for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from home to the homeless life,

the unsurpassed culmination of the holy life,
he realized it for himself with direct knowledge in this very life, and entering upon it, he dwelt in it.
"Birth is destroyed,
the holy life has been lived,
what had to be done has been done,
there is no more coming to any state of being." Thus he knew.
And the venerable Bhāradvāja became one of the Arahants.
